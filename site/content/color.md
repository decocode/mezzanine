Every website or app uses different colors to do certain jobs. One color might be for the background behind everything on the screen; another for the text so it's easy to read. A color for the main button you're meant to press; or red for when you need people to stop (just like traffic lights).

Mezzanine gives each of those jobs a name, like "background" or "primary button", and every part of the system asks for the job rather than a particular color.

Think of it like decorating a house. The plan says "front-door-color" and "kitchen-wall-color", but whoever moves into the house gets to choose the actual paint.

Every website or app that uses Mezzanine picks its own paint colors for each job. So while a button or form works in exactly the same way, it can look entirely different. If your website is about fruit, you could choose zesty colors. If your app is about space, you might go with dark ones.

Mezzanine comes with a set of its own colors, just so nothing is ever left unpainted.

## The jobs

### Backgrounds

- **Background**: the color behind everything, like the walls of a room.
- **Surface**: things that sit on top of the background, like cards and the boxes you type into. Think of it as the furniture against the wall.
- **Inverse surface**: a dark patch on a light page (or a light one on a dark page), used for small pop-up labels that need to stand out.

### Words

- **Text**: the words you read most of the time. It needs to stand out clearly against the background.
- **Muted text**: quieter words, like captions and hints, that you can skim past.
- **Inverse text**: words sitting on one of those dark patches.
- **Link**: words you can click to go somewhere else.

### Lines

- **Border**: outlines and dividing lines that separate one thing from another, like the frame around a picture.

### Buttons

- **Primary button**: the most important button on the screen, the one you're most likely meant to press, like "Buy now" or "Send".
- **Secondary button**: a less important choice that sits alongside it, like "Save for later".
- **Words on buttons**: each button gets its own text color, chosen so the words are easy to read against it.

Every button color also has slightly different shades for when you move your mouse over it and when you press it, so you can tell it's listening.

### Messages

- **Danger**: usually red, for errors and things you can't undo, like deleting something.
- **Warning**: usually orange or amber, meaning "be careful".
- **Success**: usually green, meaning "that worked".
- **Information**: usually blue, meaning "good to know".

### Focus

- **Focus**: the outline that shows where you are when you move around a page using a keyboard instead of a mouse. Lots of people rely on it, so it always needs to be easy to see.
